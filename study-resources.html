STUDY STRATEGIES

How I can learn more effectively

Effective learning is not simply about spending more hours studying. It is about using those hours purposefully, checking my understanding, and adjusting my approach when something is not working.

01

Active recall

Close my notes and try to explain a concept from memory. Answer practice questions before checking the correct responses.

Why it helps: It reveals what I understand and what needs more revision.

02

Spaced repetition

Review important material over several days instead of attempting to learn everything immediately before an assessment.

Why it helps: It encourages repeated engagement with information over time.

03

Practice problems

Work through exercises in statistics and other quantitative subjects, showing each step and checking mistakes carefully.

Why it helps: It develops reasoning and helps turn theory into practice.

04

Focused study sessions

Choose one clear objective, study without unnecessary distractions, and take short breaks when needed.

Why it helps: It gives each study session a clear purpose.

05

Teach what I learn

Explain a concept in simple language as if I were teaching another student. Return to the material when my explanation is unclear.

Why it helps: It helps identify gaps in understanding.

06

Reflect and improve

After assignments or tests, identify what went well, what caused difficulty, and what I should change before the next assessment.

Why it helps: It turns feedback into a plan for improvement.

PERSONAL ACCOUNTABILITY

My study-session checklist

Use this checklist as a simple guide each time you sit down for a focused study session.

  • Choose one specific learning objective.
  • Gather the relevant notes, textbooks, and resources.
  • Put away unnecessary distractions.
  • Practise recalling or applying what I have learned.
  • Write down questions or concepts I still find difficult.
  • Summarise the main lesson from the session.
  • Decide what to revise or practise next.
