COLLABORATION • COMMUNICATION • GROWTH

Growing Through Teamwork.

Exploring how people develop from individuals working together into a team with shared responsibilities, mutual understanding, and a common goal.

THE THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

Tuckman's stages of group development

Bruce Tuckman's model explains four original stages of team development: Forming, Storming, Norming, and Performing. Adjourning was added later to describe the conclusion of a group's work.

01

STAGE ONE

Forming — Getting started

During forming, group members become familiar with one another, discuss the task, and begin identifying the group's objectives. Members may be uncertain about their responsibilities and how decisions will be made.

My reflection: In a real group project, I would consider how our team established its goal, clarified expectations, and decided how to divide responsibilities. Clear communication at this stage can prevent misunderstandings later.

Personalise with an example: How did your group first organise itself?

02

STAGE TWO

Storming — Managing differences

During storming, differences in opinions, expectations, or working styles may become noticeable. Members might disagree about priorities or how a task should be completed. These challenges can affect progress if they are not handled constructively.

My reflection: This stage highlights the importance of patience, adaptability, and respectful disagreement. I can contribute to a healthier group environment by listening carefully, explaining my reasoning, and focusing on the shared objective instead of treating every difference as a personal conflict.

Personalise with an example: Did your group experience different opinions or approaches? How were they addressed?

03

STAGE THREE

Norming — Building cooperation

During norming, group members develop clearer expectations, establish ways of working, and begin trusting one another. Responsibilities become more organised, and communication can become more effective.

My reflection: I recognise that successful collaboration depends on accountability. Each member should understand their responsibilities, communicate progress, and respect agreed deadlines. Being dependable helps build trust and makes it easier for a team to work towards a common result.

Personalise with an example: What agreements, routines, or responsibilities helped your group?

04

STAGE FOUR

Performing — Working effectively

During performing, a team works towards its objectives with greater coordination and independence. Members understand their roles, solve problems together, and focus on completing the task effectively.

My reflection: Effective teamwork requires more than dividing work between people. Individual contributions must fit together into a coherent final result. Reviewing one another's work, sharing useful information, and helping when difficulties arise can improve both the process and the quality of the outcome.

Personalise with an example: How did your group combine individual contributions and check the quality of its work?

05

STAGE FIVE

Adjourning — Reviewing the experience

Adjourning occurs when the group's task has been completed and members prepare to conclude their work. It provides an opportunity to evaluate the outcome, recognise contributions, and identify lessons for future collaboration.

My reflection: At the end of a project, I would evaluate what went well, what could have been improved, and how effectively the group managed its time and responsibilities. Reflecting on both successes and difficulties can help me become a more thoughtful and responsible team member.

Personalise with an example: What did you learn from completing your group task?

PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT

What I want to bring to a team

01

Communication

Share ideas clearly, listen to other perspectives, and raise concerns respectfully.

02

Accountability

Take responsibility for assigned tasks and communicate early when support is needed.

03

Adaptability

Remain open to feedback, changing circumstances, and approaches that improve the team's work.

04

Shared success

Recognise that a strong outcome depends on cooperation, respect, and the quality of everyone's contribution.

MY KEY TAKEAWAY

A successful team is built, not assumed.

Tuckman's model reminds me that effective collaboration develops over time. By communicating openly, accepting responsibility, responding constructively to differences, and learning from feedback, I can contribute more effectively to academic projects and future professional teams.
